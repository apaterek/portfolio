Visual Anthropology · PhD Proposal

Temporality and the Phenomenal Field The Social Aesthetics of Seafaring

Before my career as a creative, I was an anthropologist.

I earned my master's in visual anthropology from the Granada Centre for Visual Anthropology at the University of Manchester. There my interests settled into one question: how our sense of truth and the senses themselves are shaped by the way we experience time. I explored it in the field in the summer of 2008, aboard shrimp trawlers in the Southeastern United States, producing an ethnographic film and accompanying text now housed at the Royal Anthropological Institute library at the British Museum.

Manchester offered me a PhD placement to carry the work further. I never accepted. Hollywood called, and I chose a different kind of storytelling. But the questions in this proposal stayed with me, and they still inform much of the critical and strategic thinking I bring to creative and experiential projects.
