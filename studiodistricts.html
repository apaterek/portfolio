Studio Districts

A studio-owned, first-run movie palace, and the district around it.

01

The Idea

This is a proposal to reinvent and reposition theatrical distribution as part of the experience economy. It is an opportunity to create the next wave of third spaces, optimize global distribution, and create the next era of cinema.

Rooted in existing IP and experiential talent, Studio Districts are a chance for a major media company to own the top of its feature film distribution funnel again, after a century of forced divestment from exhibition. This is leveraged to create a new kind of destination entertainment and retail space.

A new form of commercial district, built with the DNA of themed attractions, anchored by the reinvention of the movie theater: The Movie Palace.

The Movie Palace is a new evolved space that delivers an experience atop a radical new business model: the vertical reintegration of first-run theatrical exhibitions within major media studios and corporations.

The film is no longer the product; the experience of going to the movies is.

02

The Opportunity

Going to the movies was the original immersive entertainment. Reframing theatrical exhibition as immersive experience moves studio IP from the diminished growth in the traditional exhibition ecosystem to a rapidly growing sector by reinventing the space where we experience movies.

Immersive experience is set to see 29.4% CAGR to 2033 (Grand View Research) while theatrical exhibition will see only 4.54% CAGR to 2031 (Mordor Intelligence).

Repositioning the experience of going to the movies as an immersive experience means capturing massive growth, while reclaiming the first experience audiences have with a studio’s product.

The addressable market is not merely box office. It is the leisure occasion.

This is accomplished through utilizing the knowledge and skills of themed attractions to reinvent the theatrical exhibition space and make it immersive in modern terms.

03

Why Now

Four unlocks, one window. A decade ago, none of these four conditions were available. None of them are permanent. That’s all changed.

  1. 01Legal

    For the first time since 1948, studio ownership of first-run exhibition is permitted.

  2. 02Real Estate

    Post-pandemic vacancy and civic appetite for third spaces and new experiences favor land assembly and incentive.

  3. 03Technology

    Show control and immersive systems have matured to reasonable capex.

  4. 04Economics

    Streaming margin pressure forces a search for differentiated theatrical value.

04

The Argument

A demand-sensing loop was severed in 1948. With the vacating of the United States v. Paramount Pictures, Inc. by the DOJ in 2020, the path to studio-owned exhibition exists and the loop is restored, within one district.

The Palace and District draw audiences in through a unique, immersive experience of cinema.

Once there, first-run exhibition controlled by the studio results in a radically transformed analytical landscape.

Instant and owned data results in dynamic distribution planning for independent exhibitors and streaming, like never before.

05

The Studio District, Defined

A branded, walkable district, ten to eighteen acres, anchored by a studio-owned first-run movie palace built and run as an attraction.

According to research by architecture firm RIOS, engaging destinations attract 28% more Gen Z visitors than traditional entertainment.

The Studio District offers a singular destination, filled with commercial opportunity and immersive experience rooted in studio IP, new and old.

Similar to sports and shopping districts thriving around the country, studio districts offer a new angle capable of capturing an even broader audience than one sports team might.

Distinctive placemaking. The identity of theme parks, through the lens of cinema, without the gate or the capex of a new park.

06

The Palace is the Ride

Three acts, nine beats. Act one is shared by all. Act two branches into four distinct journeys. Act three returns everyone together.

The axial design of themed attractions is applied to the cinema, creating narrative immersion that is novel while carrying the familiar feel of beloved immersive attractions and narrative spatial design.

07

The Setup

The first step in changing theatrical exhibition is changing the experience from the first step inside the Palace.

The industry is attempting to solve 2026 problems with the 1996 multiplex. Seat sales still lag pre-pandemic levels, and the experiential space of moviegoing must evolve to better recover. The transformation begins immediately in the Palace through narrative design and instant immersion in the studio brand.

08

The Show

True immersion in cinema unlike anything before it. The show begins before the first frame of the movie, with unique themed dining and beverage spaces. Rotating activations bring new technology interaction to the films and prototype broader strategy for wide release.

Narrative tracks separate and drive audiences forward, giving unique experiences for each film exhibited in act two.

Similar to immersive park dining like Oga’s Cantina in Star Wars: Galaxy’s Edge, audiences can share in food and excitement as the immersion builds anticipation.

Immersive pre-shows prime audience for the films that they came to see.

09

Exit Through the Gift Shop

Exclusive merchandise and consumer products. Capturing the retail inflection point every other movie-going experience misses. Unique retail space becomes marketable content via social media, as exclusivity drives demand.

10

Vertical Corrections Applied

With first-run ownership, screen allocation can reset within initial theatrical release. Immediate corrections benefit the Palace, the Studio District, and the information informs better theatrical distribution with independent exhibitors.

11

What Studios Recapture

The same two dollars, the same six stages. Owning the flagship is how the studio gets most of it back. Studio builds the guest relationship first with its films, captures new data. Parks solved this for IP. Studio Districts do this for a new sector at fractional capex.

12

Choice and Structure

Space is programmed to provide choice of experience, while comforted by the structure of the stories, brands, and IP of studios that customers know. Boundless access to retail, dining, entertainment, and green spaces.

The Studio District is designed to be a new experiential space for entertainment and leisure. Unlike traditional theme parks, there is no ticketing or boundaries.

Dense retail, dining and entertainment spaces meld with public green space to offer spaces for interaction, community building, live events, and further studio activations.

Designed with modern transit in mind, entrances and flow come from nearly all angles and foreground the Movie Palace that is the district’s heart.

13

The Flywheel

The economic flywheel of Studio Districts maps perfectly to Disney CEO Josh D’Amaro’s ‘Disney Flywheel’. The business model of a leading studio, attraction, consumer products company made real in physical space for audiences to enjoy every visit.

Adding a new moviegoing experience adds another dynamic layer on top of the established theme park product.

14

The Pilot: Disney, Kansas City

Walt’s home before Hollywood becomes the first location in the new chapters of both moviegoing and immersive experiences. Tier One Flagship, ten to eighteen acres. Multiple phased construction with expansion built in.

15

The Alignment is Real

Six stakeholders, six categories. Everyone gets benefit, no one gets everything. Even exhibition partners, globally and in the district’s territory, will gain data insights to execute the best distribution plans, and grow the culture of moviegoing in the area.

16

A Healthier Category

Vertical reintegration will not challenge independent exhibition’s survival. Evidence shows the sector will perform better for all.

Historical data from the vertical dis-integration of film theaters in the 1950s shows that strong verticals actually supported markets where independents competed. US counties that lost studio-run competitions saw the greatest decline in independent exhibitors. (Hanssen & Melo, 2025)

Las Vegas, present day, provides a prime example of category lift from competing entertainment options.

Drawn from the total theatrical category, not just the studio’s own revenue. Every exhibitor gains from the wider line.

17

The Ask

Authorize a pilot district study for one market.

Build the new third space of cinema.
